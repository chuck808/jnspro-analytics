# Leaderboards & Benchmarking

---

## Where this actually stands right now

Worth saying plainly, up front: the leaderboard is in early access. Your own personal bests and session stats shown on it are real and accurate. The cross-rider comparison — where you actually rank against everyone else — is still filling in as more riders join, so what you see there today is closer to a preview of the mechanics than a finished ranking. That's genuinely disclosed on the page itself, not something buried in fine print.

![Leaderboard page](/docs/leaderboard-page.png)

What's already fully real and working: the opt-in system, the privacy protections, and the filtering. Those don't need a big comparison pool to function correctly — they're ready now, for whenever the rider pool catches up.

---

## You're invisible until you opt in

By default, your data stays completely private — not on the leaderboard, not visible to anyone. To appear, go to **Settings → Leaderboard & Competition** and turn it on. There's no partial state: until you flip that toggle, you don't exist on the leaderboard at all.

Opting in shares your display name, your best performance metrics, your session count, and broad age/experience brackets. **Your real name is never shown** — pick your own display name or let the system generate one (things like "FastRider2847"). Nothing more specific than that goes public: no email, no exact age, no location, no individual session details. You can opt out at any moment and you disappear from public rankings immediately — your actual data stays in your account either way, untouched.

## Filters

Metric (Reaction Time / Peak Speed / Consistency), time period (All Time / This Month / This Week), age group, and experience level — and they combine. You might be #67 globally for reaction time, top-of-your-age-group for the same metric, and mid-pack this specific week. All three can be true simultaneously; they're measuring different slices of the same underlying data, not contradicting each other.

Age and experience filters exist for an obvious reason: comparing a 14-year-old's reaction time to a 28-year-old's is fighting biology, and comparing someone on their fifth session to someone on their two-hundredth isn't a fair read on either of them. The filters let you compare against people actually in a similar position.

---

## What it's for, and what it deliberately isn't

This exists to give you honest context — not points, not badges, not a reason to feel bad about where you stand. Percentile matters more than raw rank: being 67th out of 150 riders (top 45%) is a very different story from 67th out of 800 (top 8%), even though the rank number looks the same. Once the comparison pool is properly populated, that's the number worth actually paying attention to.
